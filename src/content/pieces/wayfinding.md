## The starting point

At a fictional neighborhood library, visitors regularly stopped just inside the door. Staff had responded by adding signs. Each sign answered a real question, but together they made the entrance feel like a test.

The project began with a simple observation session: where did people pause, what did they look for, and when did they ask for help?

## What changed

The team prototyped three entrances using paper signs, movable furniture, and temporary floor markings. In one version, a welcome desk moved into the visitor’s natural line of sight. Most of the explanatory text disappeared.

### A deliberately ordinary detail

The largest change was a small table. It had been occupying the place where a visitor needed to stand to understand the room. Moving it made the next action easier to see.

> An environment can answer a question before anyone has to ask it.

## What the team learned

The fictional pilot led to the idea “Clarity can be subtraction.” This page tells the project’s story; the Idea page articulates the observation independently.

The observation later informed the Field Guide at a different organization. That connection is a specific sequence in this demonstration, not a claim about Drew’s work.
